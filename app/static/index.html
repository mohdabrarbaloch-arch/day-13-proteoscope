What this is

Proteoscope takes a protein sequence and returns three things: a secondary-structure assignment, a contact map, and a 3D C-alpha trace. It is deterministic — the same sequence always produces the same output — and explainable — every number traces back to a published scale or a stated heuristic.

What this is not

It is not AlphaFold. There are no learned weights and no network calls. Secondary structure uses Chou-Fasman propensities (1974), which agree with experiment on roughly 60–65% of residues — useful, but not a substitute for a real structure. The 3D trace is a physically-constrained model, not a prediction of the true fold. Treat the output as a teaching and triage aid.

How it works

  1. Parse & validate — clean the input, reject non-standard residues.
  2. Properties — molecular weight, pI by bisection, GRAVY, aliphatic index, extinction coefficient.
  3. Secondary structure — Chou-Fasman nucleation and extension, helix > sheet > turn.
  4. Contacts — propensity scoring with a hard physical reachability constraint.
  5. 3D trace — ideal geometry, then position-based dynamics to satisfy contacts and remove clashes.
  6. Confidence — a heuristic in [0,1] from secondary structure, contact satisfaction and clashes. Not pLDDT.
